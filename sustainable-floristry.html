Our approach

Think differently about your flowers.

Most wedding flowers fly further than the honeymoon. Ours travel about twenty miles. This page is what "sustainable floristry" actually means in practice — from how a stem is grown to what happens to it after the last dance.

Weddings & events, rethought

Beautiful doesn't require imported.

The imported model designs first and forces the flowers to comply — the same roses in February as July, flown in, foam-mounted, cellophane-wrapped. We work the other way round: your date decides your palette. A bouquet built from what British fields are doing that very week has a character no catalogue can fake — and it arrives fresher, opens better and lasts longer, because it was cut days ago, not weeks.

  • Seasonal design proposals, honest about each month's offer
  • Moss, willow and chicken-wire mechanics — never foam
  • Reusable crates and vases; no cellophane, minimal single-use plastic
  • Arrangements composted or returned to the farm afterwards

Why now

The case has never been stronger.

Fragile supply lines

Imported flowers depend on long, energy-hungry cold chains and air freight that recent years have shown to be fragile and volatile. A farm forty minutes away doesn't have that problem.

Rising costs

Freight, fuel and materials keep climbing — and imported flower prices climb with them. Local seasonal stems step around the most volatile links in the chain.

British farming needs the demand

Every stem bought from a UK grower is direct support for British farming diversification — an industry that's often overlooked, and that we think deserves better.

Honesty clause

What we don't claim

Sustainability in floristry is full of vague promises; we'd rather be specific. We don't claim carbon neutrality, we don't certify what we can't verify, and in deep winter we tell couples plainly what Britain can't grow — then design something sculptural and seasonal instead of quietly importing. If we ever supplement beyond our verified growers, we say so.

And we don't grade out character. The imported trade rejects any stem that bends; we see the bent stem, the petal marked by rain, the blossom that came a week early — and find the beauty there. Acceptance, not settling. We buy the whole honest crop and design with it, so the waste we avoid isn't just plastic.

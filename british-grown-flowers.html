Provenance

Every stem has an address.

Most florists can't tell you which country their flowers grew in. We can tell you the field. The majority of our stems are grown to order at one family-run farm in Oxfordshire — and every grower we buy from is named and verified.

Image — Anna's flower field, Wardington
Portrait — Anna Brown among the beds
Principal grower

Anna Brown — Wardington, Oxfordshire

A family-run flower farm in the north Oxfordshire countryside near Banbury — around forty minutes from our Stratford studio. Anna grows the backbone of everything we do: sweet peas and cornflowers in June, achillea and cosmos through high summer, dahlias into the autumn.

Because we plan weddings and workshop terms with Anna at sowing time, much of what we use is quite literally grown for the occasion it ends up at. Couples are welcome to visit the farm and see their wedding growing.

Field to florist

How it works

Planned at sowing

Each season's weddings and workshops shape what goes in the ground months earlier.

Cut to order

Stems are cut at the farm days — sometimes hours — before they're needed. No cold-chain warehouses, no airport.

Twenty miles, not two thousand

One short journey from Wardington to Stratford-upon-Avon, in water, in reusable crates.

Conditioned & arranged

Flowers arrive fresher than any import can be — which is why British stems open better and last longer in the vase.
